NAP Consistency: Why Your Name, Address and Phone Must Match Everywhere

Chris · 5 Jul 2026 · 6 min read

NAP stands for Name, Address, Phone number — and how consistently those three things appear across the web is one of the quieter reasons AI systems and Google get "unsure" about a business and simply leave it out of an answer altogether.

Why mismatches happen in the first place

Nobody sets out to create inconsistent listings. It happens gradually: you move office and update Google but not the five directories you signed up to years ago, you switch phone provider, a franchise listing uses "Smith & Sons Plumbing Ltd" while your Google profile says "Smith & Sons Plumbing," or an old "trading as" name lingers on a listing nobody remembers creating.

Individually, each one looks harmless. Together, they're a mess that both search engines and AI models have to try to reconcile.

Why it matters more for AI than it used to for search

Traditional search mostly cared whether your details matched between your own website and Google. AI systems go further — they cross-reference facts about a business across multiple independent sources before treating that business as trustworthy enough to cite. Conflicting information doesn't just look untidy; it actively lowers confidence, and low confidence means the AI plays it safe and recommends someone else instead.

How to audit yours

  1. Search your exact business name in quotes, plus your town.
  2. Work through the first 10–15 results and directory listings that mention you.
  3. Note every variation of your name, address and phone number you find — including old addresses, old numbers, and near-miss spellings.
  4. Don't forget the big platforms specifically: Google Business Profile, Apple Maps, Bing Places, Facebook, and any trade-specific directories relevant to your industry.

How to fix it

Start with the highest-traffic listings first — Google, Apple Maps and Bing will matter far more than a directory nobody's visited since 2019. Claim or update each one to match a single, consistent version of your details exactly, right down to how you abbreviate "Street" or format your phone number. Then work down the list. It's tedious, one-time work, but it removes a source of doubt that's otherwise invisible to you and quietly working against you.

The bottom line

Pick one exact version of your name, address and phone number, and make every listing on the internet say exactly that. It costs nothing but time, and it removes a genuine reason AI systems have to hesitate.
