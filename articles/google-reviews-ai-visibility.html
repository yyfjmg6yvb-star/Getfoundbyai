Reviews & Reputation

Why Google Reviews Are Now the Key to Getting Found by AI

Chris · 21 Jul 2026 · 6 min read

Reviews now account for roughly 16–20% of local pack ranking weight — up from 16% just a few years ago — and every major AI assistant uses your star rating as a hard cutoff before it will even consider recommending you. This isn't a "nice to have" anymore. It's one of the clearest levers you actually control.

The cutoffs are stricter than most people assume

Google Maps will still surface businesses rated 3.5–4.0★ if they're nearby enough — proximity does a lot of the heavy lifting there. AI assistants are less forgiving:

  • ChatGPT — typically only recommends businesses averaging 4.3★ or higher.
  • Perplexity — sits around 4.1★.
  • Gemini — around 3.9★.

A business hovering at 4.0★ might do perfectly well on Google Maps and still get skipped entirely by ChatGPT. That gap catches a lot of otherwise solid businesses out.

It's not just the number

Star rating is only part of it. What tends to matter just as much:

  • Recency and velocity — a steady trickle of new reviews reads as "currently trusted." A pile of reviews from 2021 doesn't.
  • Response rate — businesses replying to 80% or more of their reviews see a measurable ranking boost. Both Google's algorithm and AI models treat a reply as proof someone's actually paying attention.
  • How you handle the bad ones — customers (and AI summarising for them) judge you more by how you respond to criticism than by how many five-star reviews you've stacked up. A calm, specific reply to a negative review often does more for trust than another generic five-star.

A system that actually works

  1. Ask at the moment of a good experience — right after the job's done and the customer's happy, not three weeks later in a batch email nobody opens.
  2. Make it a one-click ask — a direct review link, not "search us on Google and leave a review," which loses most people at the first step.
  3. Reply within 48 hours — good or bad. Consistency is more valuable than eloquence here.
  4. Never argue in public — acknowledge, offer to sort it offline, move on. The reply is being read by the next hundred people who see that review, not just the one who left it.
The bottom line

If your average sits below 4.3★, that's not a vague "could be better" — it's a specific reason ChatGPT is quietly leaving you out. Reviews are one of the few signals almost entirely within your control.
