The headline

You've already done the hard part. Nobody's tidying up after you.

Here's the honest starting point: S & R Motors has a genuinely excellent reputation. 389 reviews on Google at 4.7 stars is not normal for a used car dealer — most independent dealers I look at are lucky to have a fraction of that, and the reviews themselves are unusually warm and specific, not generic five-star padding.

The problem isn't the business. It's that the version of S & R Motors that shows up depends entirely on where someone looks — and right now that's inconsistent. Some directories send people to the wrong address. Some show nothing at all. And there's a similarly-named competitor with a noticeably lower rating that Google puts right next to you when people search. None of that reflects what's actually happening on your forecourt, and all of it is fixable.

This report goes through exactly what I found, why each thing matters, and what I'd do about it — in plain English, no jargon.

What's working

What you're already doing right

Worth saying clearly: a lot of businesses I look at are starting from a much worse position than you. Here's what's genuinely good.

389 Google reviews at 4.7 stars, built up over years, with the same details showing up again and again in customers' own words — being left alone to look round without pressure, being handed the keys to test drive solo, and the flowers and chocolates on collection that dozens of customers mention completely unprompted. What's costing you customers

What's quietly working against you

None of this is about anything you're doing badly — it's about gaps that build up naturally when nobody's job is to keep every listing tidy. Here's exactly what I found.

Issue

Two different addresses floating around online

Your actual showroom is 170 Swindon Road, SN3 4PN — that's what's on Google and Facebook, and it's correct. But your Yell.com listing shows 31 Bridge End Road, SN3 4PD instead. That's your registered company address from Companies House, not where the cars are. A handful of other older directories carry the same wrong address.

Why it matters: Google, Bing and AI assistants all cross-check your name, address and phone number across the web to decide how much to trust a listing. When they don't match, it's a genuine ranking signal against you — and worse, a customer who trusts Yell over Google could genuinely turn up at the wrong building.
Issue

Your Yell listing has zero reviews and a hidden phone number

Despite 389 reviews sitting on Google, your Yell.com page shows none, and the phone number is hidden behind a "show number" click rather than being visible straight away. Anyone who finds you through Yell first sees an empty, half-finished-looking page — a completely different impression to the one Google gives.

Why it matters: Yell still ranks well for local searches and is one of the sources AI assistants pull from when they're asked "who's a good used car dealer near me." Right now it's actively undermining a reputation you've spent years building elsewhere.
Issue

A similarly-named competitor sits right next to you on Google

When I looked you up on Google Maps, the "people also search for" panel puts "SR Motor Group" directly alongside you — a completely separate, unrelated business with a 3.7 star rating from 74 reviews. I know there's nothing that can be done about the name itself, but right now there's nothing actively working to keep the two businesses clearly separated in a customer's mind either.

Why it matters: Some proportion of people searching "SR Motors Swindon" are going to land on, or get confused with, a business rated over a full star lower than you. The fix isn't the name — it's making sure every listing, bio and profile is unmistakably "S & R Motors Greenbridge, 170 Swindon Road" so there's no room for mix-ups, and keeps you visibly ahead in that comparison.
Issue

No sign of replies to reviews

Across the reviews I could see, I didn't find evidence of the business replying — to the five-star ones or the small number of critical ones. A couple of reviews mention things not going smoothly (a slow resolution on a repair, one dispute over what was promised on a car) and, as far as I could see, those are sitting unanswered.

Why it matters: A reply doesn't need to be defensive or long — it just needs to exist. AI assistants and human shoppers alike read your responses to negative reviews as the real test of a business, often more than the negative review itself. Right now you're leaving free trust-building on the table.
Issue

Facebook reach doesn't match your following

1,000 followers is a solid base, but the recent post I saw had single-figure engagement. That's not unusual on its own — Facebook's algorithm is stingy with organic reach for every business — but it suggests posting is happening without much of a plan behind it.

Why it matters: A page that's active but not really landing with its audience is wasted potential — you're already paying the "cost" of running the page (staff time to post) without getting the return.
